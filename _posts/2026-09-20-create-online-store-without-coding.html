Ten years ago, to create an online store you either hired a developer or learned to be one. Today that is the least common route. Most stores are created without writing code, using one of four approaches — and the right one depends on how much you want to own your storefront, how your customers pay, and how much you are willing to spend each month. This article lays out the options honestly, including their downsides, and explains when a dedicated online e-commerce builder is the sensible choice.

Option 1: Sell on a Marketplace

Marketplaces are the fastest way to reach buyers who are already searching. You list products inside someone else's store and they handle discovery, payments and often delivery.

The trade-off: you pay a commission on every sale, compete side by side with identical products, and never own the customer relationship. Your "store" is a listing page you cannot brand. Marketplaces are a good first test of whether a product sells — and a poor long-term home for a brand.

Option 2: Sell From Social Media

A large share of small sellers start here: post products on a Facebook page or Instagram, take orders in messages, deliver with cash on delivery. It costs nothing and conversations convert well.

The trade-off: there is no catalog customers can browse on their own, no automatic order record, and every sale needs a back-and-forth about price, size and address. It does not scale past a certain number of daily messages, and none of it is visible to search engines. The common next step is to keep social media for marketing and add a proper store link customers can order from — which is exactly the gap store builders were designed to fill. See turning a Facebook page into a website for a worked example.

Option 3: General Website Builder With a Shop Add-on

Tools like Wix, Squarespace and WordPress (with WooCommerce) let you build any kind of website and then bolt on e-commerce. If your brand needs a blog, a portfolio, booking pages and a shop, this is the flexible choice.

The trade-off: the shop is one module among many, so setup is longer and there are more moving parts. WooCommerce in particular requires hosting, plugin updates and occasional troubleshooting — not coding, but not effortless either. If you only want to sell products, you are paying in time for flexibility you may never use.

Option 4: Dedicated E-commerce Builder

A dedicated online store builder does one thing: it gives you a hosted storefront with a catalog, product pages, an order flow and a dashboard. Sign up, add products, share the link.

The trade-off: design freedom is limited to what the themes allow, and moving to another platform later means exporting and re-importing. For most sellers that is a fair exchange for going live in an afternoon and never thinking about hosting.

When an E-commerce Builder Makes Sense

Choose a builder if most of these are true:

  • Your main goal is taking orders, not publishing content.
  • You already have customers (on social media, in person) and need a link to send them.
  • You want a browsable catalog with categories, photos and stock.
  • You want orders recorded in one place instead of scattered across chats.
  • You do not want to maintain hosting, plugins or updates.
  • You want a predictable yearly or monthly cost.

Choose something else if: you need a very specific custom design or checkout flow (custom development), your product is a commodity where marketplace traffic is the whole game, or your site is primarily editorial with an occasional product.

What "No-Code" Actually Looks Like in a Builder

People sometimes imagine a no-code e-commerce website still involves dragging blocks around for hours. In a dedicated builder it is mostly filling in forms:

  1. Register — you get a store at a subdomain within a minute.
  2. Add categories and products — name, price, photos, sizes, stock. Same as filling a spreadsheet.
  3. Pick a theme — choose a preset, upload logo and banner.
  4. Set delivery and payment — cash on delivery, a gateway, delivery charges.
  5. Share the link — orders appear in the dashboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I create an e-commerce website without coding at all?

Yes. Marketplaces, social selling and store builders all require zero code. Only fully custom stores do.

Is a no-code online store taken seriously by customers?

Customers judge a store by its photos, prices, clarity and how quickly you respond — not by how it was built. Many well-known brands run on hosted builders.

Can I start on social media and add a store later?

That is the most common path. Keep the social channels for reach and use the store link for the actual ordering.

What does it cost to create an online store this way?

From free (social, marketplaces with per-sale fees) to a flat yearly license on a builder, up to hundreds per year on mainstream platforms. Full cost breakdown here.
