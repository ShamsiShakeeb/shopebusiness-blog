Four reasons Square Online struggles in Thailand

1. Square payment processing isn't offered in Thailand

Square Payments operates in a short list of countries — the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, Japan, Ireland, France and Spain at time of writing. Thailand is not on it. You can design a Square Online site, but you cannot take a payment through it the way Square intends.

2. No PromptPay, no native cash on delivery

Thai buyers expect to scan a PromptPay QR or pay the courier at the door. Square's checkout is card-first; COD and local QR flows are not part of the product. Sellers end up asking customers to "just message us on LINE", which defeats the point of a store.

3. The free plan isn't really yours

On Square Online's free tier your site displays Square advertising, sits on a Square-branded URL and cannot use a custom domain. Removing ads means moving to a paid monthly plan — recurring dollars, billed whether you sold anything that month or not.

4. A multi-page checkout built for cards

Cart → account → shipping → billing → card. Each screen is a place where a Thai mobile shopper on 4G drops off. Local buyers want one form: name, phone, address, done.

How a COD store actually runs in Thailand

From link to delivered in four steps

  • Customer orders on your Shop-E-Business page — name, phone, address. No card, no account.
  • You confirm on LINE or by phone if the address needs a condo building or room number.
  • You ship with Flash Express, Kerry Express or J&T Express, using COD collection if you want the courier to take the cash.
  • You get paid in banknotes or a PromptPay scan at the door, then mark the order Delivered in your dashboard.

Who Square Online is still right for

If your business is registered in a Square-supported country, your buyers pay by card, and you want Square POS hardware in a physical shop, Square is a coherent ecosystem. For a Thai seller shipping COD parcels from Bangkok, Chiang Mai or Phuket, it solves the wrong problem.
