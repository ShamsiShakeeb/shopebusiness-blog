You can build an e-commerce website online in a single day if you prepare the right things before you touch any software. This guide walks through the process in order — the decisions, the content you need, the setup inside a store builder, and the checks to run before you share the link. It assumes you are using a hosted e-commerce builder rather than writing code; if you are still weighing that choice, that article covers it.

Before You Start: What You Need Ready

Most stores that stall do so because the owner opens a builder with no content prepared. Have these on hand:

Launch checklist

  • A store name and a short one-line description of what you sell.
  • A logo (even a simple text logo) and one banner image.
  • A list of products: name, price, short description, stock, and sizes or colours if relevant.
  • At least one clear photo per product, ideally on a plain background.
  • Your delivery area and delivery charge(s).
  • How customers will pay: cash on delivery, bank transfer, mobile wallet, card gateway.
  • A phone number or messaging link customers can reach you on.

Step 1: Decide What You Sell and to Whom

Write one sentence: "I sell [products] to [who] in [where], and they pay by [method]." This sentence decides most of your setup. A seller shipping handmade jewellery nationwide with cash on delivery needs a very different order flow from one selling digital templates by card worldwide. It also tells you which builder features you actually need — and which you can ignore.

Step 2: Prepare Your Product Content

Put your catalog in a spreadsheet before entering it anywhere. Columns: name, category, price, stock, sizes/colours, short description, photo filename. This makes data entry fast and lets you spot gaps (missing prices, products without photos) before they become live pages.

Photos

Phone photos are fine if they are sharp, well lit and consistent. Use the same background and crop for every product so the catalog looks intentional. Save files with descriptive names such as blue-linen-shirt-front.jpg; they double as alt text hints later.

Descriptions

Two to four sentences: what it is, what it is made of or does, who it suits, and anything a customer would ask before ordering (dimensions, care, delivery time). Avoid copying manufacturer text — write it in your own words.

Step 3: Choose an E-commerce Builder and Sign Up

Choose the builder against your one-sentence description from Step 1, not against feature lists. The main questions:

  • Does it support how your customers pay? Cash-on-delivery stores need a simple order form; card-first stores need the right gateway.
  • Is the storefront good on phones?
  • Is the total yearly cost predictable?
  • Can you add products quickly?

Sign-up on most builders gives you a store on a subdomain immediately. On Shop-E-Business, for example, registering creates yourshop.shopebusiness.com and opens the admin dashboard straight away; the free trial needs no card.

Step 4: Add Categories and Products

Create categories first — it is easier to place products into existing groups than to reorganise later. Keep the list short; five to eight categories is plenty for most small catalogs.

Then add products from your spreadsheet. For each one:

  1. Enter the name, price and category.
  2. Upload photos (first photo becomes the thumbnail).
  3. Add sizes or colours if the product has them, with stock per variant if possible.
  4. Paste the description.
  5. Set stock quantity so sold-out items are handled automatically.
  6. Flag a few products as featured so the home page has something to show.

Add ten products, then stop and look at the storefront. Fixing a formatting habit after ten products is quick; after a hundred it is a chore.

Step 5: Set Up Your Storefront Design

Pick a theme that matches your product type. Editorial themes suit fashion and accessories; clean grid themes suit groceries, electronics and everyday goods. Upload your logo and banner, set brand colours if the builder allows it, and stop there — most design time beyond this point does not increase sales. Shop-E-Business ships three presets (Classic, Fashion Luxe and Grocery); the theme customisation guide shows what each looks like.

Step 6: Configure Ordering, Payment and Delivery

This is the step that decides whether orders actually reach you.

  • Cash on delivery: customers fill in name, phone and address; you confirm by phone or message. Simplest to start and dominant in many markets.
  • Payment gateway: connect the gateway your customers use. Check per-transaction fees and payout timing.
  • Delivery charges: set them clearly, ideally by area (inside city / outside city). Surprise charges at checkout are the main reason people abandon an order.
  • Order statuses: understand how the dashboard moves an order from new to delivered so you can keep customers informed.

Step 7: Test the Store on a Phone

Open your store on your own phone — not on the desktop where you built it. Browse a category, open a product, and place a real order from start to finish. Check that:

  • Photos load quickly and are not cropped badly.
  • Prices, sizes and stock are correct.
  • The order form is short and the button is easy to tap.
  • The order appears in your dashboard with all the details you need to fulfil it.

Ask one friend to do the same and tell you where they hesitated.

Step 8: Set SEO Details and Launch

Before sharing the link, write a page title and meta description for the home page and your top products. Use plain language a customer would search: "Handmade leather wallets in Dhaka — free delivery inside city" beats "Home". If the builder supports custom URLs, set readable slugs for products. The ranking guide covers this in detail for Shop-E-Business stores.

Then launch. Put the store link in your Facebook page, Instagram bio, WhatsApp status and any customer messages. Your first orders will almost always come from people who already know you.

After Launch: The First Two Weeks

  • Reply to every order fast; speed builds trust for a new store.
  • Add products you were unsure about — the store makes it cheap to test.
  • Note the questions customers ask and add the answers to product descriptions.
  • Watch which products get views but no orders; often the photo or price needs work.

Common Questions

How long does it take to build an e-commerce website online?

With content prepared, a store of 10–30 products can be live in a few hours. Preparation (photos, descriptions) usually takes longer than the setup itself.

Do I need a domain name to start?

No. Builders provide a subdomain. You can move to your own domain once the store is proven.

Do I need coding skills?

No. Everything above is done through forms and settings. If you want to compare approaches, read how to create an online store without building everything from scratch.

What will it cost?

Depends on the platform and whether you add a domain or paid gateway. Our cost breakdown goes through each item.
